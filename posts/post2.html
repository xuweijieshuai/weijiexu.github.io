Recent Advances in Topic Modeling with LLMs

February 25, 2025

Topic modeling has traditionally relied on statistical methods like Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), but the landscape is rapidly changing with the introduction of Large Language Models (LLMs). In this post, I'll discuss how our DeTiME approach leverages diffusion models and encoder-decoder LLMs to achieve state-of-the-art results in topic coherence and interpretability.

The Evolution of Topic Modeling

Classical topic modeling approaches like LDA work by assuming documents are generated from a mixture of topics, with each topic represented as a distribution over words. While these methods have been successful, they often produce topics that lack coherence or contain unrelated terms, making interpretation difficult.

Neural topic models improved upon these limitations by incorporating word embeddings and deep learning architectures. However, they still struggled with producing truly interpretable topics, especially for specialized domains with complex terminology.

Enter LLMs and Diffusion Models

Our DeTiME approach takes advantage of the semantic understanding capabilities of LLMs combined with the generative power of diffusion models. This hybrid approach allows us to:

  1. Capture more nuanced semantic relationships between words
  2. Generate more coherent and interpretable topic descriptions
  3. Adapt to domain-specific terminology more effectively
  4. Produce hierarchical topic structures that better represent complex document collections

One of the key innovations in our work is the use of diffusion models to gradually refine topic representations. Starting from random noise, the model iteratively denoises the representation until it converges on a coherent topic structure. This process is guided by the semantic understanding provided by the encoder-decoder LLM.

Practical Applications

We've successfully applied DeTiME to several real-world scenarios:

  • Analyzing customer feedback and reviews to identify emerging concerns
  • Organizing research literature to identify trending topics and gaps
  • Structuring internal documentation to improve knowledge management

The results consistently show improvements in topic coherence and human interpretability compared to traditional methods. For organizations dealing with large volumes of text data, this approach offers a more effective way to extract actionable insights.

In future work, we plan to extend DeTiME to handle cross-lingual topic modeling and to incorporate multimodal information for even richer topic representations.
